ONEHEALTH LENS - FIVE-MINUTE WINNING DEMO SCRIPT
================================================

Purpose
-------
This is a competition-ready script, not a product tour. Tell one complete
story: a community observation becomes a trusted, explainable intervention.
The spoken script is approximately 700 words (about five minutes at 140 wpm).
Bracketed instructions are presenter actions and are not spoken.

Demo principle
--------------
Never narrate what the audience can already read. Point to the proof, explain
why it matters, and move the story forward. Use one high-risk site consistently
throughout the demo.


00:00-00:25 | THE HOOK: FROM SIGNAL TO DECISION
------------------------------------------------
[Start on Overview with seeded data loaded. Pause for one second before
speaking.]

"A stream can look normal while its water, biodiversity, and human exposure
risks are moving in the wrong direction. The problem is not a lack of data.
The problem is the gap between an observation and a decision.

OneHealth Lens closes that gap. It turns a community water observation into
an explainable, auditable One Health action - connecting ecosystem condition,
animal health, and human health in one workflow."

Judge signal: Mission impact, problem clarity, memorable positioning.


00:25-00:50 | OVERVIEW: ONE SHARED PICTURE
-------------------------------------------
[Point to the hero, monitored segments, and One Health matrix.]

"The Overview gives every stakeholder the same starting point. A volunteer
sees plain-language context. A scientist sees the monitored catchment. A
municipal team sees where attention may be needed.

The important design choice is traceability: every headline can lead back to
the observation and the evidence behind it. This is not a static dashboard;
it is a shared picture of catchment health."


00:50-01:25 | DASHBOARD: PRIORITISE THE NEXT ACTION
----------------------------------------------------
[Open Dashboard. Show KPIs, risk distribution, search/filter, and select the
same high-risk site.]

"Now I move from context to operations. The Dashboard shows monitored sites,
risk distribution, validation status, and the highest-priority segments.

I can search and filter instead of scanning a spreadsheet. I select this
high-risk site and move directly to its evidence. The operational question is
simple: not 'what data do we have?' but 'which site deserves attention first,
and why?'"

Judge signal: Usability, practical municipal value, decision speed.


01:25-02:15 | IMPACT CARDS: EXPLAIN THE SCORE
----------------------------------------------
[Open Impact Cards. Select the same site. Show Detailed, then briefly switch
to Simple and back to Detailed.]

"This is the product's explanation layer. The Impact Card shows the risk
index, risk band, confidence, causal chain, diagnostic triggers, and
recommended protocol. It does not ask the user to trust an unexplained
number.

The score combines four evidence domains: water quality at 30 percent,
biological health at 30 percent, human exposure at 20 percent, and
environmental pressure at 20 percent. The interface presents that model as
ecosystem, animal, and human pillars so it remains understandable outside a
data science team.

For a quick field briefing, Simple mode shows the status and plain-language
summary. For scientific or municipal review, Detailed mode reveals the
triggers and actions. The same result serves both audiences.

Speech playback supports hands-free access, and the FHIR preview shows how an
insight can move toward health-system interoperability."

Judge signal: Innovation, explainable AI, accessibility, technical depth.


02:15-02:45 | TRENDS: DISTINGUISH SIGNAL FROM NOISE
---------------------------------------------------
[Open Trends. Point to historical risk, forecast, confidence, and submission
frequency.]

"A single reading should not dictate a policy decision. Trends add context:
is this catchment stable, improving, or deteriorating?

Here the historical trajectory, forecast baseline, confidence, and citizen
submission frequency appear together. The system shows not only a signal, but
how much evidence supports that signal. That encourages better sampling and
more responsible intervention."


02:45-03:15 | ONE HEALTH: CONNECT THE DOMAINS
----------------------------------------------
[Open One Health. Point to the matrix, alert protocol, and action/recovery
sections.]

"One Health makes the connection visible. Water degradation can reduce
habitat quality and biodiversity while changing exposure pathways for people.

This page translates those relationships into an alert state, recovery
context, and action protocol. It does not replace experts. It gives
volunteers, researchers, and health teams a common, reviewable evidence base."


03:15-03:40 | MAP: TURN RISK INTO PLACE
----------------------------------------
[Open Map. Show the legend and select the same site or its nearby cluster.]

"Risk is geographic, so the Map turns the result into place. The markers use
the same risk bands as the Dashboard and Impact Cards.

A catchment manager can identify a cluster, select a site, inspect its causes,
and choose the next response: confirm with sampling, remove pollution,
restore habitat, or coordinate public-health follow-up."

Judge signal: Impact, spatial prioritisation, real-world feasibility.


03:40-04:15 | FIELD CAPTURE: NO DATA BLACK HOLE
------------------------------------------------
[Open Field Capture. Show the form, media attachment area, and connectivity
indicator. If available, demonstrate the queued/offline state.]

"The loop begins in the field. A volunteer records the site, measurements,
rating, and optional photo or video evidence using a guided form.

If connectivity drops, the submission is queued locally in IndexedDB, with a
fallback for supported browsers. When the connection returns, it can retry;
the PWA also prepares failed submissions for Background Sync. The visible
connectivity state tells the user what happened.

That is a small UX detail with a major impact: a difficult field visit does
not disappear into a data black hole."


04:15-04:45 | AUDIT TRAIL: MAKE TRUST INSPECTABLE
-------------------------------------------------
[Open Audit Trail. Expand one validation stage and one FHIR/storage stage.]

"Finally, I open the Audit Trail. It exposes the pipeline from ingest and
normalisation through validation, enrichment, correlation, FHIR mapping,
insight rendering, storage, and distribution.

I can move backwards from this risk card to the original evidence, quality
flags, transformation steps, and interoperable representation. That makes
the result reviewable and reproducible rather than merely impressive on
screen."

Judge signal: Technical implementation, governance, scientific credibility.


04:45-05:00 | CLOSE: THE WINNING CLAIM
--------------------------------------
[Return to the strongest Impact Card. Hold the screen; do not navigate again.]

"OneHealth Lens is a translation layer: from community observation, to
explainable risk, to a coordinated One Health response.

It is accessible enough for a volunteer, transparent enough for a scientist,
and structured enough for a catchment manager. The next proof points are
deliberately measurable: official OAH profile validation, calibration against
real field and laboratory references, and PostgreSQL performance under
concurrent load.

We are not claiming those results before testing them. We built the system so
they can be tested, audited, and improved."

Final judge signal: Honest evidence, scalability mindset, confidence.
