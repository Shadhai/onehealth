ONEHEALTH LENS - FIVE-MINUTE TOP-TIER DEMO SCRIPT
==================================================

Target delivery: 4:45-5:00 at a confident 130-145 spoken words per minute.
Bracketed text is a presenter action and is not spoken.


00:00-00:25 | OPEN
-------------------
[Start on Overview with seeded data visible.]

"Water data is collected everywhere, but it is rarely converted into a
decision people can trust. OneHealth Lens closes that gap.

It turns a citizen observation into an explainable, auditable One Health
decision. In one workflow we connect water quality, biological health, human
exposure, and environmental pressure - then return the result as an action
card and an interoperable FHIR record."


00:25-00:55 | OVERVIEW
----------------------
[Point to the hero, matrix call-to-action, monitored segments, and narrative.]

"Overview is designed for a mixed audience. It answers: where are we
monitoring, what is changing, and why does it matter for health?

The interface starts with plain language, but every summary remains connected
to the underlying observation. A community member can understand the story;
a scientist or manager can continue into the evidence."


00:55-01:35 | DASHBOARD
----------------------
[Click Dashboard. Show KPI cards, risk distribution, search, then search for
Segment 4 and click a row.]

"The Dashboard is the operational view. KPIs show monitored sites and risk
distribution. Priority cards surface where attention is needed first.

The table is searchable, sortable, and filterable by risk and validation
flags. I can search for Segment 4, compare its status, and open the exact
impact card from the row.

This reduces the journey from 'something may be wrong' to 'this is the site,
this is the evidence, and this is the next action.'"


01:35-02:20 | IMPACT CARDS
--------------------------
[Click Impact Cards, select a segment, then click Detailed.]

"This is the explanation layer. The Impact Card shows the One Health Risk
Index, risk band, confidence, causal links, diagnostic triggers, and
recommended actions - not just a black-box label.

The transparent model uses four domains: 30 percent water quality, 30 percent
biological health, 20 percent human exposure, and 20 percent environmental
pressure. The UI summarizes these as ecosystem, animal, and human pillars so
the result stays understandable.

Detailed mode exposes the reasons behind the score. Speech playback supports
hands-free field use, and FHIR preview connects the insight to health-system
interoperability."


02:20-02:55 | TRENDS
--------------------
[Click Trends. Point to forecast, risk history, confidence, and frequency.]

"A single observation can be noisy. Trends show whether a catchment is stable,
improving, or deteriorating.

The page combines citizen timestamps with optional weather context, shows a
seven-day forecast baseline, and displays confidence and submission frequency.
That is responsible analytics: the system shows both the signal and how much
evidence supports it, so teams know when more sampling is needed."


02:55-03:25 | ONE HEALTH
------------------------
[Click One Health. Point to the matrix, alert state, recovery/action sections.]

"One Health makes the connection explicit. Water degradation can affect
habitat and biodiversity, while changing exposure pathways for people.

This page synthesizes human, animal, and ecosystem evidence into one
structured explanation with alert protocols, recovery windows, and export
actions. It does not replace experts; it gives communities and experts the
same traceable starting point."


03:25-03:55 | MAP
----------------
[Click Map. Show markers and legend; select a site if tiles are available.]

"Risk is spatial, so the Map places monitored sites in their catchment
context. Marker colors use the same risk index as the dashboard and cards.

That supports a practical municipal workflow: identify a cluster, select a
site, inspect the evidence, and decide whether the next step is sampling,
cleanup, habitat work, or public-health follow-up."


03:55-04:25 | FIELD CAPTURE
---------------------------
[Click Field capture in the header. Show measurements and media fields.]

"The workflow also works where the data is collected. A volunteer can record
site, pH, dissolved oxygen, temperature, rating, and optional photo or video
evidence.

When connectivity is poor, the observation is stored locally in IndexedDB
with a fallback, then retried when the connection returns. The PWA registers
Background Sync for failed submissions, and the header makes connectivity
visible. This prevents a field submission from becoming a data black hole."


04:25-04:50 | AUDIT TRAIL
--------------------------
[Click Audit Trail and expand one or two stages.]

"The Audit Trail is the trust layer. It exposes the nine stages: ingest,
normalize, validate, enrich, correlate, FHIR mapping, insight rendering,
storage, and distribution.

I can move backwards from a risk card to its validation flags, enrichment
context, original payload, and FHIR representation. That makes the result
reviewable, reproducible, and institution-ready."


04:50-05:00 | CLOSE
-------------------
[Return to Overview or the strongest high-risk Impact Card.]

"OneHealth Lens is more than a water dashboard. It is a translation layer
from community observation to explainable, auditable, and actionable health
intelligence - accessible to volunteers, useful to scientists, and practical
for catchment managers.

The next validation step is clear: official OAH package validation, real
field and laboratory calibration, and measured PostgreSQL deployment
performance. The product is built so those claims can be tested, not guessed."
