The data model

Citizen observations combine water, channel, biological, assessment, and location context.

Water

pH, dissolved oxygen, temperature, TDS, conductivity, nutrients, pharmaceuticals, and coliforms.

Channel and margins

Channel form and modification, connectivity, bank condition, and riparian vegetation.

Biological

Macroinvertebrates, diatoms, fish, macrophytes, amphibians, birds, and invasive organisms.

Assessment

Overall rating, degradation score, media, WGS84 coordinates, timestamp, and contextual notes.

How the score is computed

The Risk Index is a transparent weighted combination, with reasons exposed on every Impact Card.

Water quality30%

pH, oxygen, TDS, conductivity, nutrients, and coliforms.

Biological health30%

Biological indicators and riparian vegetation.

Human exposure20%

Contamination, pharmaceuticals, smell, and appearance.

Environmental pressure20%

Weather context, channel modification, and bank condition.

0.00 - 0.24 LowHealthy conditions.
0.25 - 0.54 ModerateStress signals require monitoring.
0.55 - 1.00 HighIntervention is recommended.

What makes this different

FHIR-native: interoperability is part of the pipeline output.

Explainable: flags and score reasons remain visible to reviewers.

Three pillars: human, animal, and ecosystem health stay distinct.

Auditable: every stage persists data for downstream inspection.

Honest: overview numbers come from the same database as the API.

Accessible: keyboard navigation, dark mode, glossary, and print support are built in.

Why this matters

Citizens collect pH, dissolved oxygen, biological indicators, smells, and photographs. OneHealth Lens gives that data meaning instead of letting it disappear after submission.

Every record is normalized, validated, enriched, correlated across One Health pillars, mapped to FHIR, and returned as an explainable impact card.
